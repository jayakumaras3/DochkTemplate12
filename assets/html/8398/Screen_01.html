Healthcare professionals rarely separate the sales representative from the company; in their eyes, you are Zydus. This means your conduct, from punctuality and appearance to how you manage confidential information, is not a personal choice but a professional obligation. Credibility is built through consistency, and in pharma sales, consistency in conduct is what earns you a seat at the table, visit after visit. 

Let’s start with a scenario. 

Jake, a new sales professional, has a 9:00 AM appointment with Dr. Patel, a busy general physician. Jake arrives 15 minutes late, blames the traffic and immediately launches into his product pitch without acknowledging the doctor’s time constraints. He also leaves a stack of promotional literature on the reception desk, visible to other patients waiting in the lobby. 

Later that week, Ryan, another new sales professional, has a similar appointment. He arrives five minutes early, waits patiently and when he meets Dr. Patel, he opens by asking how his week has been before requesting two minutes of his time. He keeps all promotional material in his bag until he is in the consultation room. 

Dr. Patel remembers both visits. He tells his staff to keep Ryan’s future appointments on the calendar. Jake does not get a second meeting. 

Conduct like Ryan’s is not about being liked; it is about being trusted with someone’s limited time and attention. Standards around punctuality, discretion, and respect for the healthcare setting exist because they protect the credibility of every representative who walks through that door after you, and the reputation of the organisation you carry with you.
